Highlighted Posts

If you read five things here, read these. They are the ideas I keep coming back to.

  • Aug 2006
    The 95 Theses of Progressive Teaching
    Fifty theses on what a teacher should be, borrowed in form from Martin Luther, from “Teachers should be the change they want to see in their schools” to “Teachers should learn.” I wrote it the summer before my third year of teaching.
  • Jun 2007
    The Ripe Environment
    “I am tired of talking about the tools.” Ten prerequisites for a place where people actually want to learn together. Everything else on this site grew out of it.
  • 2007 · 43 comments
    Safety vs. Panic
    My students were collaborating on a wiki with eighth graders in Connecticut, until one parent's complaint got every one of those kids removed overnight. I asked who failed. 43 people answered, and the conversation is still one of the best this blog has had.
  • Dec 2010
    Question 365 of 365: What is enough?
    The last of a year of questions. “My year hasn’t been about numbers. It has been about ideas. And thoughts. And change.”
  • Feb 2022
    Why I Left Public Education (and then came back)
    “Scale is a seductive myth.” Why I left a school district for a startup in 2010, why I stayed less than a year, and what I believe about change now.

In 2010 I wrote one question a day for a year, 365 of them, about technology, education, and community, and answered each one in public.

Why I write

I write when I need to write. I write for myself and I write for my future. I write to help uncover the right work, and to make sure my blind spots are not too large. I share it because someone might stumble across it and find it useful, and because twenty years in, the writing and the person are the same thing.
