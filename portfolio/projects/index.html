Featured · Teaching

Human Shaped

Some problems are computer-shaped. You can hand them to an AI agent and be done. Learning, community, and relationships are not, because they only happen when a person does the work. I want folks who build with AI to be able to tell the difference, and to build software that takes the mechanical work off people's hands and leaves them the learning.

Human Shaped is how I am teaching that, and it comes in three parts. A method: the Universal App Template, nine stages from why you are building all the way to shipping on every store, written where your agent will read it. A free five-week course, run in cohorts from humanshaped.org, where every learner builds one real app for people they can actually name, and shows it to the rest of us. And a short set of principles an app can answer, one by one, to say it is human-shaped.

The cohorts meet in Google Meet. So, I built a Meet add-on that turns the call into a classroom: a run of show everyone follows, questions answered live on the main stage, a classmate's app opened right in the call for everyone to try, breakout rooms, and a replay beside the recording for anyone who missed it. It never scores or ranks anyone. Leaders see everyone's work, and each learner sees their own. Everything a dozen real calls taught me went back into the template, so the next person does not have to learn it the hard way.

Civic · Passed

Yes on 4A: Tell the Story of Our Schools

4A passed. Ballot Issue 4A, the $10 million mill levy override for Littleton Public Schools, won with about 55% of the vote, in a year when the overrides next door in Jeffco and Douglas County were trailing on election night. I led the social media team for Citizens for LPS, the volunteer committee behind it. We started an Instagram account from zero on September 23, painted a whole lot of car windows (CBS Colorado noticed), and learned that throwback posts about folks' own years in LPS beat anything abstract. One of them reached about 10,000 views.

I also built yeson4a.org, a storytelling toolkit for volunteers: story prompts by who you are, a Share Studio, a cost calculator that shows the county assessor's actual math, and a source behind every fact. Now that the campaign is over, the whole thing is a template the next campaign can copy, along with an honest retrospective of what worked and what did not.

Data & Simulation

Harvey Cup Draft Assistant

A fantasy-football projection engine, draft simulator, and live advisor that runs inside the Yahoo draft room. Blends four sources' stat projections, scores them under the league's own rules, values against a simulated replacement level, and picks a strategy by running thousands of drafts and seasons scored on title odds. Every clever rule was tested, and most of them lost. Twenty-six mock drafts and two real ones, every pick graded against the advice.

Infrastructure

CI Fleet & Device Harnesses

Archive Watch's content pipeline is 30+ scheduled GitHub Actions workflows that discover titles, audit rights, check that every file plays, and publish one catalog to every app. Keeping that fleet honest meant building the compute/apply lock split, budgets that publish, a self-healing sweeper, a health auditor where a red X is reserved for broken, and a product-health dashboard that reads every store's metrics. On the device side: scenario harnesses that drive real Apple TVs, Pixels, Fire TVs, and Rokus and read the screen back with OCR, so a feature ships when it has been seen working. All of it is documented in the Universal App Template.

How I decide what to build

Every project starts with a real need, usually mine. I build tools that solve one specific problem for students, for teachers, or for me. I build most of them with Claude, and I still decide what is worth building and whether it is done. I want each one to work, to be open, and to be something someone else can learn from or build on.
