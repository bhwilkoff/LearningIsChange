# Scaling up a flawed system…

[![Combines :Image:Nuvola apps ksim.png, :Image:N...](http://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/38/Icons_example.png/202px-Icons_example.png)](http://commons.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image:Icons_example.png) Image via [Wikipedia](http://commons.wikipedia.org/wiki/Image:Icons_example.png)

I wrote a single thing on the back of a paper at a meeting today:

[Scaling](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaling_%28geometry%29) a system that exists vs. Creating a different system that includes networked learning.

What I meant by this I am not exactly sure (nor can I talk about all of the good and hopeful things that transpired during the meeting, either). All I know is that we shouldn’t be looking to make our current teaching model work in more and bigger ways. We should not be extending traditional [pedagogy](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogy) into online environments. We should not be taking something that no longer works for one school and then trying to emulate it for all schools.

I guess it makes sense to say this as well:

Just because a system can scale doesn’t mean that it should. And just because a system can’t scale, doesn’t mean it isn’t valuable to someone. While I believe that all of our learning should be [scalable](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scalability), that doesn’t mean that we need to have every learner doing the exact same thing. Creating multiple entrances makes sense. There is no one [ring to rule them all](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Ring). We need spaces that work for every *single* one, not “everyone”.

Forgive my obtuse discourse tonight. I am having trouble with getting concrete at the moment. I hope you glean some meaning from the words. If not, I will try again tomorrow.
